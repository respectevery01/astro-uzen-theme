Travel apps spent a decade optimizing for speed. Fast booking, fast check-in, fast transfers, fast itineraries. The goal was to minimize friction and maximize throughput. Turn travel into a logistics problem and solve it with software.

A counter-movement is gaining ground. People are choosing to stay longer, travel slower, and see less. They want one city in two weeks, not five cities in seven days.

## The experience economy

The shift mirrors what happened in coffee. First wave: instant, cheap, fast. Second wave: latte culture, brand loyalty. Third wave: single origin, pour-over, the barista knows your name.

Travel is entering its third wave. The differentiator is not price or speed. It is depth.

## What this means for design

Slow travel does not mean slow websites. It means the website should communicate depth — rich content, long-form storytelling, photography that does more than fill space.

The booking flow can still be fast. But the discovery phase — browsing destinations, reading stories, imagining the trip — should feel unhurried. Give people room to wander.
