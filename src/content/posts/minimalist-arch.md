For a decade, every website looked the same. Same rounded corners. Same gradient hero. Same sans-serif type. Same three-column card grid. The internet became a airport lounge — comfortable, identical, forgettable.

Brutalism pushed back.

## The appeal of ugly

Brutalist web design borrows from 1960s architecture: raw materials, heavy forms, zero ornament. On the web, that translates to exposed grids, system fonts, harsh borders, and color combinations that would make a brand guidelines document weep.

It feels honest. A brutalist site does not pretend to be your friend. It presents information and gets out of the way.

## When it works

Brutalism works when the content is strong. If your writing is good, brutalist typography amplifies it. If your writing is bad, no amount of gradient mesh will save you.

It fails when it becomes performance art — sites so aggressive in their ugliness that they become unusable. Accessibility is not optional just because your aesthetic is raw.

## The middle ground

The best designers right now are not pure brutalists. They are taking the principles — bold type, high contrast, honest structure — and applying them with enough polish to remain usable. That is where Margin sits.
